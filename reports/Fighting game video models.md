# Fix the misses before buying pixels

The best next gains come from retraining on the 1,700 replays, fixing how openers are attributed to a side, and teaching the transcript to see moves that make no contact. A bigger backbone or more pixels will not deliver them. **The case for higher resolution is weak**: raising source or upload resolution has no support, and raising model input has one confounded, slightly negative test on our data plus gains of about 2-4 points per 2x linear in other domains, mostly where subjects are tiny. Saved test outputs show that **whiffs and interrupted moves are 39% of transcript events but 70% of its exact-move misses**, while close starts, the headline suspect, sit in only 10% of misses and moves with under 20 training starts in only 4%. On the opener chain, about half the error is detection rather than move naming, so no move-network upgrade can lift 0.887 / 0.855 past about 0.937 / 0.932 by itself. I expect retraining to reach about 0.90 / 0.88 on openers, and the transcript head and feature work to add about 0.02-0.05 exact-move F1, though the transcript figure rests on analogy. The largest product gap is that the transcript does not run on the CPU server at all, so a small per-frame spotter trained from the logs matters more than any ViT. In-domain self-supervised pretraining is a later bet: we are far outside the label-scarce regime where it pays, and no published study tests it on game footage.

**How to read the evidence.** Each claim carries one of four tags. **[Ours]** is a measurement already recorded in this repo or its results files. **[Ours, new]** is a count I computed for this report from saved test outputs (`exp_tr_ens5grid_test_preds.parquet`, `transcript_events.parquet`, `results_errors_v2_*.json`, `results_v2_chain.json`); these reproduce the published 33,981 test events, 0.57 whiff, 0.64 interrupted and 0.47 close-start figures, and pooled F1 0.728. **[Other domain]** is published work on sport, real video or still images. **[Inference]** is my reasoning or arithmetic, untested. I found no peer-reviewed work on frame-precise move recognition from fighting-game video, so nothing external is a direct benchmark. Many paper figures were read through a page-summarising tool, so check them against the paper before relying on one for a large spend.

## Half of the remaining error sits where backbones cannot reach

The opener chain loses 11.3 points on old replays and 14.5 on new. **[Ours, new]** Those split into 5.5 / 6.2 points missed by the detector, 0.8 / 0.7 dropped as false starts, and 5.1 / 7.7 points of wrong move (`results_errors_v2_old.json`, `results_errors_v2_new.json`). Every backbone experiment so far competes only for that last slice. The most useful detail is inside the misses: **115 old and 65 new missed openers had a detection nearby in time on the other side**, which is 2.4 / 2.5 points of chain. That is more than the gap between the fine-tuned V-JEPA 2 and the ResNets (0.018 / 0.020) ([README](../tools/ggst_logger/README.md)). Some of these are real opposing events such as trades, so 2.4 points is a ceiling. Another 19-22% of misses have no logged HP drop, which is partly a labelling limit.

Three further slices stand out. **[Ours, new]** Projectile openers lose 92 old and 87 new openers (2.0 / 3.3 points), mostly as wrong move, and older logs are known to miss object credit, so part of this is label artefact. On new replays, 23 wrong moves come with the wrong character (0.9 points); `hud_chars.py`, in the working tree and not yet committed or scored, reads the HUD portrait and name, which do not change with player colour, and should recover most of them. The rare catch-all covers 165 / 119 openers at 0.485 / 0.454 right against 0.902 / 0.874 for moves with their own class, so even bringing it level is worth **1.5 / 1.9 points at most**, and that scores picking `(rare)`, not naming the move.

The transcript's shape is different and more surprising. **[Ours, new]** Of 33,981 test events, 10,416 are exact-move misses at ±4, and about two-thirds of those have no prediction of any class in range.

| Event group (pooled test fold) | Share of events | Found, any class | Right move | Share of misses |

Rows overlap, so shares do not sum. **[Inference]** Three conclusions follow. Short cancelled moves are probably close to unobservable: a move logged for under 10 rows barely appears on screen, and these account for a quarter of misses, so 1.0 F1 is not the ceiling. Long whiffs are the largest visually recoverable group, and they are a modelling gap, not a sharpness gap: a 30-row whiff occupies the same crop as a 30-row hit, yet lacks the hit spark, HP drop and combo counter. And the mid-frequency band (50-199 starts, 0.66 right against 0.77 for 200-999) carries half the misses, which is an argument for more replays, not long-tail tricks. By arithmetic, raising long whiffs from 0.54 to 0.70 right adds about **+0.022 F1**, while bringing every close pair to 0.70 adds about **+0.010**. Timing has a similar limit: exact-move F1 at ±8 is only 0.015-0.017 above ±4 **[Ours]**.

## Spotting research says fix rate, heads and reasoning, not architecture

The published recipe is consistent. A tiny 2D CNN with temporal shift runs on every frame at 224-448 px, trained end to end, with a light sequence model and a per-frame softmax ([E2E-Spot](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2207.10213), [T-DEED](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.05392v2), [AdaSpot](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.22073)). **[Other domain]** End-to-end beat frozen Kinetics features by 6-17 mAP at δ=2, the closest published match to our ±67 ms, but it beat features fine-tuned on the same dataset by only -0.6 to +6.4 mAP. Removing the sequence model and shifts together dropped Tennis from 96.1 to 60.1 at δ=1, while the choice of shift module moves results 1-3 mAP and adding flow gains 0.0-1.7 ([E2E-Spot](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2207.10213)). These all run on real sport at 25-30 fps. **[Inference]** The result that transfers is that domain fine-tuning does most of the work, and our transcript still uses frozen tokens. On openers, fine-tuning gave +0.025 / +0.043 chain over frozen in four epochs with a NaN **[Ours]**, which is why step 8 has the larger expected transcript gain of the backbone options.

Do not expect an architecture swap to show a clear win. The same E2E-Spot model on Tennis at δ=0 is reported as 71.6, 69.78, 51.42 and 45.34 across four papers ([E2E-Spot](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2207.10213), [AdaSpot](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.22073), [MFS](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.07381), [ASTRM](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.00147)), which is larger than most differences between recent models. The SoccerNet 2025 winner kept T-DEED and went from 51.72 to 60.03 by merging heads, pretraining on 500+ matches, adding augmentation and tuning ([report](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.19182v1)). That is a 1 s tolerance on soccer, but the lesson on where the points came from is direct. Cheap head changes do pay in that literature: Soft-NMS over NMS took T-DEED from 81.27 to 85.15 and 68.40 to 71.48 at δ=1 ([T-DEED](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.05392v2)). No paper measures recall against the gap between events, or two events in one output bin, so our harness has to answer this itself.

The research notes disagree on how much of the 0.47 close-start recall is structural, and the saved data settles the size of the prize. **[Ours, new]** 1.7-1.8% of events share a tubelet with an earlier start of the same side, at any of the eight tubelet phases I tried. Two slots can therefore add at most about that much recall, and part of the loss comes from the merge distance of 8, which is the edge of its tested grid **[Ours]**. So the head fix is worth doing because it costs an afternoon, not because it unlocks much. Rate is the same story. F3Set lost 14.4 F1 points from stride 2 to 4 at ±1 frame, at about our sampling rate if stride means source frames ([F3Set](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.08222v2)), which argues for 15 Hz tokens. But our own 60 fps DINOv2 test moved a refiner within ±1 by +0.036 and within ±2 by -0.008, close to the ±1 label error **[Ours]**, and anything the app re-encodes for upload is 720p30. Run 15 Hz after the head round, or a gain cannot be attributed. Nothing I found shows a benefit above 30 fps, and nothing tests it directly.

F3Set's other headline does not transfer cleanly. Factorising 1,108 flat classes gave +6.5 F1evt, plus 2.4 from context, and its localiser reached 86.7 against 40.3 for full events ([F3Set](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.08222v2)). **[Inference]** Our character mask already leaves about 36 candidates per side, and exact move trails category only by 0.08 at ±4, so we have the opposite problem: detection, not naming. Separate button and category heads already exist. Treat factorisation as a modest head-round option, and don't budget the F3Set gain.

## ViTs belong in training, and the server needs a much smaller student

The published leaderboard favours V-JEPA. Frozen V-JEPA 2 ViT-L reaches 73.7 on SSv2 against a prior best of 69.7 ([repo](https://github.com/facebookresearch/vjepa2)), and distilled V-JEPA 2.1 ViT-B (80M parameters) scores 74.5 / 85.1 on SSv2 / K400, above undistilled ViT-L at 74.2 / 84.3 ([V-JEPA 2.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.14482)). Our own order agrees among frozen models, 0.844 / 0.792 for V-JEPA 2 against 0.811 / 0.764 for DINOv2 and 0.780 / 0.729 for frozen ImageNet ResNet18, but **the fine-tuned CNN ensemble still wins on our data** at 0.887 / 0.855 **[Ours]**. The closest public analogue also says a CNN is competitive: on rendered fonts, ImageNet ResNet-50 hit 98.8%, DINOv2-B with LoRA 98.7%, and full DINOv2 fine-tuning 93.4% at 50 epochs ([font paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.13889)). It is one paper on an easier still-image task. No source compares these backbones after fine-tuning on a small dataset, so the ranking must come from our runs.

The current fine-tune result is a floor, not a verdict. It ran four epochs at about 64 minutes each and 9.4 GB, and its loss went NaN through most of epoch 2 before recovering **[Ours]**. **[Other domain]** Float16 overflow is a documented cause in ViT training, fixed by bf16 in published cases, and loss spikes follow gradient spikes driven by stale AdamW second moments in the patch embedding ([stable low-precision training](https://arxiv.org/html/2304.13013)). That does not show it is our cause, since a learning-rate or gradient spike fits equally well, so reproduce under fp32 first. Then use bf16 without a GradScaler, gradient clipping or StableAdamW, β2 of 0.99, layer decay about 0.75 ([VideoMAE](https://arxiv.org/html/2203.12602)), drop path 0.2, no flips, and 20-30 epochs. Layer decay of 0.75 and LoRA are the two cheap alternatives to "last 8 blocks". Train on dense move starts this time, because the current fine-tune was never trained on whiff labels. **[Inference]** I'd expect parity with the ResNets at best on openers: 50/50 averages of frozen backbones with v2 stayed within noise, and they lowered the rare bucket **[Ours]**. The transcript is where a domain-tuned encoder has the most room.

Serving is a separate problem. **[Inference]** A ViT-L pass is implausible on the shared server: its FP32 weights alone are about 1.2 GB, and the transcript needs three views. [MFS](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.07381) lists a ViT at 3,522 GFLOPs against 42-46 for RegNet-Y spotters, which is not a like-for-like clip but shows the scale. Distilled V-JEPA 2.1 ViT-B is still 80M parameters at 384 px and needs its weight licence confirmed, so it is a candidate teacher, not a server model. Published distillation costs are modest for a moderate size step: -1.2 to -3.2 on SSv2 from ViT-G down to L and B ([V-JEPA 2.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.14482)), and -6.2 from ViT-g to S ([VideoMAE V2](https://github.com/OpenGVLab/VideoMAEv2/blob/master/docs/MODEL_ZOO.md)). I found no paper that distills a video ViT into a CNN and reports both accuracy and CPU speed, so step 7 is original work with no published error bar.

Two points change the priorities here. First, the production chain is not the 0.887 chain. The server runs `--light` at **0.878** on 720p30 on 4 threads, in 4 min 15 s and 4.4 GB per 3-minute match **[Ours]** ([ROADMAP](../tools/ggst_logger/ROADMAP.md)). One research note assumed 2 threads; confirm the real allocation. Distilling the ensemble into that ResNet18 is the cheapest production gain available: on CIFAR-100, distillation kept about 72% of a 10-model ensemble's gain ([Allen-Zhu and Li](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.09816)), which is **[Other domain]** and small-image work, so treat +0.007 as a target. Second, the RegNet spotter is both research branch and student. The student's labels need no teacher at all, since the logs give exact starts for every frame, and teacher outputs add soft targets once cross-fitted, which ROADMAP point 6 notes do not yet exist for the training replays. Decode is already the limit on the transcript's feature pass (62 s wall, 21 s GPU) **[Ours]**, so count decode in the budget and test a direct 720p decode instead of today's upscale to 1080p60.

Do not plan around INT8. On ResNet-50 it ran **1.76x slower** on a Core i9-10900K without VNNI and 2.11x faster on a Cortex-A78AE with dotprod ([portability study](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.16085)), at a cost of about 0.12 points; those are single-run values with thread counts unstated, and OpenVINO's documentation points the other way for non-VNNI CPUs ([FAQ](https://docs.openvino.ai/2024/about-openvino/performance-benchmarks/performance-benchmarks-faq.html)). Check the CPU flags, time FP32 in ONNX Runtime and OpenVINO on the server, and only then try static INT8.

## Tail, colour and self-supervision each have a low ceiling here

**Long tail.** Benchmarks show large tail gains, but on balanced test sets and with head losses we'd feel: Balanced Softmax on iNaturalist moves head / tail from 75.3 / 60.4 to 70.9 / 70.4 ([survey](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.04596)). On the one large long-tailed video benchmark, gains were about 1-2 points ([VideoLT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.02668)). **[Inference]** Our metric weights moves by frequency and masking already shrinks the problem, so expect macro F1 (0.615 / 0.612 at ±4) to move and micro F1 to barely change. Post-hoc logit adjustment is the right first try because it needs no retraining and its temperature can sit on a dial ([Menon et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.07314)). Avoid class-balanced sampling in representation training, which fell below Softmax on ImageNet-LT, and don't stack re-balancers ([survey](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.04596)).

Our true tail is under 5 examples and has no classes at all, which makes it a data question. Same-engine generation looks promising by analogy: on camera traps with a real render gap, 120 synthetic images cut rare-class error 28% with under 0.5% cost elsewhere, while 1.4M images raised other classes' error 13% ([Beery et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.05916)). Whether training mode or CPU matches give a match-like HUD and context is unverified, and only real replays should test it. Before any recording, step 9 is free of new footage: the transcript's vocabulary has 1,215 moves against the opener net's 904 **[Ours]**, and it may already hold examples of what openers lack. Deterministic animation is the most original idea and the least evidenced. I found no measured work on it. A few-hours kNN test of current embeddings on test openers against training openers, split by rare moves, would show whether it deserves more. Prototype heads were the worst option on iNaturalist ([Kang et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.09217)), so don't assume.

**Colour.** No study measures palette swaps, and **our own data has no measured colour gap** yet. The analogy favours plain jitter: on Flowers-102, whose hue-shift average sat at 13.41, jitter reached 68.44, and colour-equivariant networks added about 2 points or less elsewhere ([Lengyel et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.19368v1)). The same paper warns that invariance is disastrous when colour is the signal (24-29% against 91% on long-tailed ColorMNIST), and Strive's Roman Cancel flash colours may be such a case, which I have not verified. Global hue rotation is also the wrong physical model for a palette swap, which recolours each material separately. So run the sweep first: 12 hue steps plus channel permutations on the saved test fold costs one evaluation pass. If colour IDs can be logged, stratifying by colour on real replays is stronger than any synthetic test. If there is a gap, try hue and channel augmentation, then per-material recolouring. Game footage adds a warning of its own: a Melee model fell from 98.8% to 47% once an on-screen input graphic correlated with its labels was removed ([Yale poster](https://yale-lily.github.io/public/s2018/BenVars_AustinLachance_presentation.pdf)), so check that the whole-frame stream is not learning a stage or a palette.

**Self-supervision on VODs.** The published 40-point gains compare with from-scratch training. Against a good transfer start the sign can flip: VideoMAE pretrained only on target clips scored 91.3 / 62.6 on UCF101 / HMDB51, below Kinetics transfer at 96.1 / 73.3 ([VideoMAE](https://arxiv.org/html/2203.12602)). Semi-supervised gains concentrate at 1-20% labels and flatten after ([TimeBalance](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.16268)), and out-of-class unlabeled data pushed FixMatch below the supervised baseline, 49.7 against 52.7 ([Su et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.00679)). With 8.2M labelled frames we are outside that regime, and VOD footage contains lobbies and possibly other game versions. **[Inference]** The argument for trying is that Strive is far from Kinetics, and domain shift is real, so expect a single-digit effect at most. The only cost anchor is 144-224 GPU-hours on 8 H100s for a ViT-B ([controlled study](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.13183)), which is not a plan for one 3090. If tried, use ExPLoRA-style continued pretraining of the last blocks ([ExPLoRA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.10973), satellite data, self-reported), mask the HUD, and sample across characters and stages rather than hours. HUD masking has one direct warning from game video: a Melee spatial autoencoder locked onto the clock and percentages rather than the characters ([Slippify](https://cs231n.stanford.edu/papers/text_file_840589945-Parsing_Super_Smash_Bros__Melee_Frames.pdf)).

The unlabeled pool has better jobs: soft-label data for the student, and, more urgently, a realistic robustness test. No tournament VOD has logged labels, so our "typical VOD" is one clean encode **[Ours]**. Re-streaming logged replays through a real path gives exact labels on realistic degradation. Published compression losses are mild to CRF 30 and -4.5 to -5.8 at CRF 40 ([Otani et al.](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2204.09166)), retraining on compressed video mostly did not help, and Gaussian-noise augmentation hurt ([Yi et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.06513)). So add compression augmentation only if the ladder shows a drop.

## The case for higher resolution is weak

**Source and upload resolution: no case.** 720p30 at 3 Mbit scored 0.888 against 0.887 at 1080p60 on the chain **[Ours]**. **[Inference]** This does not show pixels are useless, only that the extra ones are discarded: a 640 px crop at 720p is still 427 source pixels, 2.2x the chain's 192 px input. The result is firm for the chain only. The transcript has not been run on 720p30 copies at all, though its 960×540 decode makes a gain from more source pixels unlikely.

**Model input resolution: weak case.** The one run that fed more pixels, ResNet18 at 224 px and 12 frames against 192 px and 8, came out slightly worse: 0.8431 against 0.8511 chain and 0.9265 against 0.9332 on normals **[Ours]**. It changes two axes at once and is only a 17% linear bump, so the evidence is thin rather than strongly negative. **[Other domain]** F3Set went from 40.3 to 44.4 F1evt between 224 and 448 px while stride 4 cost 14.4 ([F3Set](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.08222v2)), and GolfDB got +4.4 for doubling cost through resolution against +8.7 through context ([GolfDB](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1903.06528)). Clear high-resolution wins are in small-object sport, such as SoccerNet Ball at 796×448 and above ([SoccerNet 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.10587)). **[Inference]** Our crops already provide that zoom, past F3Set's top setting in pixels per source pixel if its sources are 1080p. Our largest spatial gain needed no new pixels: 2×2 tokens gave +0.038 / +0.039 F1 with intervals clear of zero **[Ours]**, which suggests any spatial loss happens after the encoder.

**The cost is real.** **[Inference]** Scaling our measured pass to 1,700 replays, 384 px takes 23-52 h and 512 px takes 42-166 h, against 10.4 h now for three views, with quality under interpolated position embeddings unknown. Training already reaches about 23.4 GB of 24 GB. It would raise CPU serving by at least 2.25x, against the direction of the roadmap's next step, and would expose more compression artefacts on VODs, which is plausible but unmeasured. Super-resolution solves missing pixels we don't have, and learned crop policies try to find characters whose positions our player locator already gives.

**Where it could still matter**, and the cheap way to find out. Button and weapon confusions in the transcript (button F1 0.712 / 0.685) are the plausible spot; whiffs and close starts are not, since neither is a sharpness problem. Projectile errors are about what the frame contains, so the scene stream's 384×216 is the sensible single thing to raise. Settle it in order: inspect 50 button errors at model input size, since if a person can tell them apart at 192 px, pixels are not the limit; run step 15's one-axis ladder, stopping if 128 px loses little; try 4×4 tokens. Test V-JEPA at 384 px on the subset only if one of those comes back positive. Do not re-extract 1,700 replays or change the upload before then.

## Conclusion

Most of the options on the table assume the bottleneck is the model's sight: better features, more pixels, finer time. Our own saved outputs point elsewhere. For openers, it is attribution, data volume and a server that runs a lighter model than the headline. For the transcript, it is moves that lack the contact cues the current features exploit, and a fifth of its events are long whiffs that should be visible on screen. Literature on sport supports that framing without proving it, since the closest benchmark has an opposite profile, with strong localisation and weak naming. So the most valuable hour left is cheap: read 100 `wrong_side` openers and 100 long whiffs by eye.

The bigger uncertainty is whether a fine-tuned encoder trained on whiffs actually closes that gap. Step 8's value depends on it, and no external source can answer it. Also unknown is the true floor from unobservable short moves, which determines how high exact-move F1 can go. A sensible stopping signal for the backbone research: if a clean 20-30 epoch fine-tune on dense starts cannot lift long-whiff recall meaningfully, stop chasing encoders and put the budget into the CPU student and more replays, where the return is already measured.
